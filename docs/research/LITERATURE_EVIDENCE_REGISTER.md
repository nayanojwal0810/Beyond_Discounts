# Literature and Evidence Register — Beyond Discounts

**Reviewed:** 17 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish what the project can credibly claim, what methods are defensible, and where the closest prior work overlaps. This is a screening register to support the final critical review, not a substitute for reading each full paper.

**Evidence labels**
- **Direct q-commerce** — studies expressly focused on quick commerce.
- **Adjacent grocery/e-commerce** — related service, loyalty or segmentation setting; transfer to q-commerce must be argued.
- **Methodological** — supports method choice or diagnostics, not consumer-behaviour claims.
- **Industry/context** — supports market framing, not academic causal inference.
- **Dataset** — documents the empirical data source, not independent findings about q-commerce.

## Cross-study synthesis: what is already known and what remains for this project

1. **The broad topic is not novel by itself.** Indian q-commerce adoption and loyalty are already examined with survey/SEM designs (references 2–5), and online grocery studies examine satisfaction, assortment and economic value (7–8). Do not claim a first study of q-commerce loyalty or price/quality drivers.
2. **The data are different from operational CRM logs.** Transaction-based segmentation studies use real recency, frequency, monetary and behavioral event histories (18–21). This project only has self-reported cross-sectional survey items; it cannot establish actual customer lifetime value, churn, repeat purchase, cart abandonment or promotional lift.
3. **Segmentation must earn its place in the recommendation.** Mixed-type distance, cluster selection and resampling stability are well-studied issues (10–15). A weakly separated but stable partition may still be commercially useless; validation must include independent outcomes and whether decisions change.
4. **CRM is about a measured decision process, not a clustering chart.** The value-creation and performance-assessment framing of Payne and Frow (9) supports requiring a clear audience, evidence, candidate intervention and real-world test metric for every recommendation.
5. **Ordinal questions should stay ordinal.** QA46 and QA47 are ordered categorical responses, not continuous interval measurements. Ordinal modelling requires explicit assumption checks; inference and prediction claims must remain separate (16–17).
6. **Applied contribution of this project:** compare never-users, previous users and the survey-defined current-user cohort; combine stated intentions with current non-use barriers and preferred improvements; then explicitly test whether unsupervised clusters add decision utility beyond those simpler lifecycle distinctions. This is a practical synthesis/application contribution, not a claim of novel statistical method.

## Final literature review standard

Before final report approval, open and read the full accessible text for the papers used for theoretical claims, verify full author names, journal, volume/pages and DOI, and record whether each was peer reviewed. The final review must be thematic and critical, not 21 disconnected mini-abstracts. Use direct q-commerce papers to establish the nearest prior work, adjacent grocery/CRM work to explain transferable constructs, and method papers to justify the statistical workflow. Industry reports can supply context but should not be used as substitutes for academic literature.
